Part 1The Chapter 6 fact sheet

Grouped by theme, from Chin's decks and the Unit 4 review deck. Learn each fact as an A→B chain, not an isolated term.

Silver & the global economy

  • American silver (Potosí) → price revolution. Spanish-colonial silver flooded into Europe and China → massive inflation. Chin's #1 question: "silver caused ___" → inflation.
  • Mercantilism = state controls trade to build national power: amass specie (silver/gold), limit imports, encourage exports.
  • VOC (Dutch East India Company) = the great joint-stock company — pooled investor capital, spread risk, state-backed. The signature economic innovation of the Netherlands and England.
  • Banda Islands = nutmeg. The VOC massacred and enslaved the Bandanese to corner the nutmeg trade — corporate violence for monopoly.

The Portuguese empire

  • Motive: direct access to Indian Ocean trade wealth — cut out the Ottoman/Muslim middlemen.
  • Method = force → a trading-post empire (Goa, Malacca, Hormuz), not conquered territory.
  • Cartaz = the permits Indian Ocean merchants had to buy from the Portuguese — a protection racket at sea.
  • Why posts, not colonies? Asian empires were too strong to conquer; Europeans only got footholds. Key cause-effect pair.
  • Monsoon winds = the environmental fact of Indian Ocean sailing.

Africa & the slave trade

  • Benin restricted participation in the slave trade for ~2 centuries — and lost territory because of it. Opting out of Atlantic trade had costs.
  • Queen Nzinga (Ndongo/Matamba): fought the Portuguese, negotiated, allied with the Dutch.
  • In Chin's EXCEPT question, the LIE is "African rulers were willing to enslave their own subjects."
  • Biggest destinations: Brazil & the Caribbean (sugar). Sugar was profitable because enslaved people were worked to death and replaced.
  • Inside Africa: more men taken → gender imbalance in families and communities.

Japan: reunification

  • Order: Oda Nobunaga → Toyotomi Hideyoshi → Tokugawa shogunate.
  • Effect: less social mobility for non-samurai — rigid class order: samurai → peasant → artisan → merchant.
  • Sword hunt (Hideyoshi) = disarmed the peasants.
  • Sankin-kōtai (alternate attendance) = daimyo spend every other year in Edo, families hostage → drained daimyo wealth, centralized Tokugawa power.
  • The lie in Chin's EXCEPT: "it restricted peasants' weapons" — that's the sword hunt, not sankin-kōtai.

Spanish Americas (review deck — fair game)

  • Encomienda = Spanish coerced Indigenous labor. (Distinct from mita, the Inca-origin rotational labor system the Spanish used in the Andes.)
  • Casta system order: Peninsular → Creole → Mestizo → Indio/Negro. Brazil more fluid; Spanish America more rigid.
  • Demographic collapse: smallpox, measles, malaria killed 50–90% of Mesoamericans — disease, not guns.
  • Columbian Exchange: Europe→Americas: cattle, pigs, horses. Americas→Eurasia: maize, potatoes.
  • Smallpox went Europe→Americas, NOT the other way.
  • Magellan (Spain, 1519) = first circumnavigation. Da Gama (Portugal) = first sea route around Africa to India.

Part 3SAQ prep

An SAQ is 3 short parts (a/b/c), 1 point each. Each part: one direct answer sentence + one sentence of specific evidence or context. No intro, no fluff, 1–3 sentences max.

Most likely Ch 6 angles, based on what Chin emphasized:

  • Silver flows — economic effects of American silver on Europe and China (price revolution; Ming currency and taxes).
  • Portuguese vs Dutch methods — force and trading posts vs joint-stock companies. Compare, don't just describe.
  • Japan — how reunification changed society (social mobility, Christianity banned, sankin-kōtai).
  • African agency — Benin's limits, Nzinga's resistance. Use specific names or it doesn't score.

Practice protocol (11 minutes, Sunday): pick one angle, write the SAQ in 8 minutes. Then spend 3 minutes scoring it: does each of a/b/c have (1) a direct answer sentence, and (2) one named piece of evidence? Any part missing either → rewrite that part once, then stop. Unscored practice just rehearses your own errors.

Part 6Chapter 5 gap repair — after Ch 6 is solid

Ch 5 was the roughest quiz (7.5/12). Do this only after the Ch 6 Kahoot is 10/10 — it's an investment in the final, not in Tuesday. The five that bit hardest, from Chin's full 14-question Ch 5 deck:

  • Yasak = tribute tax on non-Russians (Siberian furs). Iltizams = Ottoman tax farming → "crazy high taxes" for regular people.
  • Russia: serfdom expanded 1600s–1700s; Peter & Catherine = westernization.
  • Akbar legitimized rule by abolishing the jizya, translating Hindu texts, creating "Divine Faith" — the lie is the "caliph + Dalai Lama" option.
  • Mughals vs Ottomans: Mughals used zamindars (taxes by land productivity), Ottomans used tax farming.
  • Balkans (multi-select!): the two FALSE statements — "Christian/Jewish women refused to use Muslim courts" (they used them) and "France allied with Austria" (France allied with the Ottomans against the Habsburgs). Conversion was limited (~19% Muslim).
